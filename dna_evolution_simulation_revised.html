Modeling DNA Changes Over Time

Why are we doing this investigation?

In previous lessons, you explored how scientists use evidence to determine how closely related different organisms are. You also examined models that show how organisms share common ancestors.

At first glance, hippos and whales might seem very different. However, scientists have determined that they share a common ancestor that lived approximately 55 million years ago.

How can organisms that share a common ancestor become so different over time?

DNA contains genetic information that influences an organism’s traits. Over many generations, changes in DNA, called mutations, can accumulate. Some of these changes affect physical traits, while others do not.

In this investigation, you will create a digital model to explore how DNA and physical traits can change over 500 million years.

Investigation question

How can changes in DNA over time help explain similarities and differences between organisms that share a common ancestor?

Learning objectives

  • Model how DNA sequences change over time.
  • Explain how changes in DNA can affect physical traits.
  • Compare the DNA sequences of related organisms.
  • Explain how DNA similarities provide evidence of common ancestry.
  • Identify strengths and limitations of an evolutionary model.

Your task

You will begin with one common ancestor and create two separate descendant lineages. Both lineages will start with identical DNA and body lengths.

Every time you advance 100 million years, the simulation will randomly select a DNA base in each lineage and determine whether it changes. You will observe how these changes affect body length according to the rules of the model.

Pay attention to both the DNA sequences and the physical traits as you progress toward the present day.

This is a hypothetical model, not a reconstruction of whale or hippo evolution. Real mutations can occur at many different times, and evolution involves populations over generations.

1. Understand the model

Both lineages begin with the same ancestor. Each time you advance 100 million years, the simulation randomly selects one DNA position and a replacement base (A, T, C or G) independently for each lineage. The replacement may be the same as the original base.

  • More Gs and Cs than before: body length increases by 1 meter.
  • More As and Ts than before: body length decreases by 1 meter.
  • No change in these totals: body length stays the same.

Remember: These are simplified model rules, not actual biological rules for body length.

Vocabulary

Ancestor: An organism from which another organism descended.

Descendant: An organism descended from an ancestor.

Lineage: A sequence of organisms connected by descent.

Most recent common ancestor: The most recent ancestor shared by related descendant lineages.
